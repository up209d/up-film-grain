# Tuning constants

## Tuning constants (all in engine.py, all calibrated by measurement)

| Constant | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `EDGE_REF` | 0.06 | Fixed edge-magnitude reference. Must stay a constant, not a statistic — see invariant 1. |
| `_GRADE_TEMP_GAIN` | 0.40 | Peak channel gain for Temperature at ±1: red and blue move this far in opposite directions, green is left alone, and the vector is then normalised against the luma weights so the control cannot also expose the frame. |
| `_GRADE_TINT_GAIN` | 0.30 | Same job, on Tint's green/magenta axis. **Smaller than Temperature's on purpose** — green carries 0.7152 of the luma weight against red's 0.2126 and blue's 0.0722, so the same magnitude costs more level. Reusing 0.40 measured a 2.3% luma drift against Temperature's own 1.8%; 0.30 brings it to 1.4%, the same envelope. |
| `_GRADE_TONE_KNEE` | 0.5 | Where the Shadows and Highlights ramps meet. Both are quintic over half the range, so every pixel is in exactly one of them and a gradient shows no seam. Not exposed — a knee and a falloff per end would be four more sliders in a section that is meant to stay cheap. |
| `_GRADE_TONE_MAX` | 0.35 | How far a tone lift travels at ±1, as a share of its headroom — **for the *expanding* half of Shadows/Highlights only** (Shadows negative, Highlights positive). **Not 1.0**: that takes a black pixel to pure white at +1 and squeezes the useful range into the slider's first tenth. Measured on a real photo, Shadows +0.5 took the mean from 0.19 to 0.53 at 1.0. The *recovering* half has no such cap on purpose: scaling a lift by the pixel's own level is precisely what made it non-monotonic, so `_tone_roll` blends to a fixed exponential shoulder and its ±1 endpoint is that curve's asymptote rather than a constant. A steeper-shoulder gain was swept and declined — 2.0 buys the frame mean 0.271 → 0.308 and drops the worst-case slope 0.184 → 0.068, i.e. it crushes the deepest blacks to buy reach. |
| `_RECON_LO` / `_RECON_HI` | 0.94 / 0.999 | Soft window over which a channel stops being a measurement and starts being a ceiling, for highlight reconstruction. Soft because the window doubles as the blend weight, and a hard threshold would draw a visible contour round every blown region. The top is short of 1.0 because an 8-bit ceiling is 255/255 and JPEG ringing puts real clipped pixels a code value or two either side of it. |
| `_RECON_MIN_EVIDENCE` | 0.02 | How much of the neighbourhood must still be a clean sample before a reconstruction is trusted, as a share. Below it the estimate fades to a no-op rather than being divided into existence — in the middle of a region blown in every channel there is genuinely nothing to recover from, and saying so beats inventing it. |
| `_RECON_CEIL` | 4.0 | Ceiling on a reconstructed value, two stops above white. The estimate divides by the local chromaticity of whichever channel survived, so a highlight lit by something that channel barely sees (deep blue under tungsten) has a small denominator and would otherwise run away. Real photographs do reach it — measured 4.000 on the repo's own test frame — so it is a working limit, not a theoretical guard. |
| `_RECON_ROLL_KNEE` | 0.80 | Knee for reconstruction's own roll, the curve that makes what it recovered visible. High on purpose: the recovered data lands just above white, so only the top fifth of the range has to give way. `_GRADE_TONE_KNEE`'s 0.5 would work and would be the wrong control — that is a broad highlight roll, i.e. `grade_highlights`, and duplicating it here would mean reconstruction quietly graded the picture as well as repairing it. |
| `_RECON_ROLL_GATE_FRAC` | 0.25 | Feather width for that roll's gate, as a fraction of the reconstruction radius; the mask is dilated by **twice** this before feathering, or the gate never saturates and the roll runs at partial strength. 0.25 beats the 0.5 first tried on every axis: swept against the second derivative of a repaired ramp, contouring starts at 0.05 (3.2e-03 against the source's own 2.2e-03) while 0.10–0.50 all sit at 1.4e-03, so 0.25 clears it 5× over — and going wider only costs, flattening the span-vs-radius curve less well and adding 300px to `pad_for` at the top of the range. |
| `_GRADE_CONTRAST_GAIN` | 0.9 | Gain on the two-way Contrast pivot at ±1, floored at 0 so it cannot invert through grey — at −1 the gain is exactly 0.1. Smaller than the deferred film curve's 1.1: this control has no shoulder to catch what it steepens, so a gentler reach delays clipping at the top of the slider. |
| `_GRADE_CLARITY_GAIN` | 1.6 | Gain on the *positive* side of Clarity only. The negative side is pinned at exactly 1.0 and must stay there: at gain 1, −1 removes precisely 100% of the band, and past that it inverts local contrast rather than flattening further. |
| `_GNORM` | 0.55 | Noise normaliser. **The old note here claimed field std ~0.27 clipping ~3.6%; re-measured 2026-07-31 it is std ~0.45 clipping ~18%**, constant across octave counts now that `_fbm` preserves variance. The 18% is pre-existing — a single-octave field measures the same — so this row was simply wrong, not broken by a change. Lowering it flattens the distribution's tails further. |
| `_AMP_SCALE` | 0.38 | Maps the 0–100 intensity slider to amplitude; default 32 lands near 3.5% luminance sigma. Was 0.5 — recalibrated when `_fbm` started preserving variance, since the old value was silently compensating for a field running at 43% strength. |
| `_MIN_CELL` | 0.8 | Floor on lattice cell size in working pixels. Below Nyquist it is pure aliasing. |
| `_SAND_TAPS` | ±2σ, 5 taps | Tangential sanding filter. Reaches ±2σ, not ±1 — contour roughness sits at longer wavelengths than it appears to, and a ±1σ filter removed only 2% of it. Weights are normalised at use: the table sums to 0.991. |
| `_BLUE_HUE` | 230.0 | Centre of the blue-compensation window, **in linear light** where the stage runs — the sRGB number is 220. Skies span 222-236 there; cyan water 194, purple shadow 249. |
| `_BLUE_SAT_FLOOR` | 0.12 | Below this a pixel is grey and the compensation leaves it alone. Without it every neutral in the frame takes a cast — the failure `vibrance` is also written against. |
| `_SCATTER_STENCILS` | 9 entries | Scatter footprints: (name, first angle, count, locus, inner, alt). **Indexed by the parameter value, which is what a preset file stores** — renumbering silently changes every preset that used one, so append rather than insert. `verify.py` pins it against `choices` in params.py name-for-name. Every entry must keep peak travel ≤ reach, which is what `pad_for` reserves for; an L∞ "square" locus would reach 1.41× and would have to be paid for. |
| `_AA_TAPS` | ±1, 3 taps | Anti-aliasing filter along the isophote. Short *on purpose*, and the opposite choice from `_SAND_TAPS` for the same underlying reason: a stair-step is a pixel-scale wobble, so reaching further only averages away the shape the contour has. To make the stage bite harder, raise the pass count — not this. |
| `_AA_PASSES` | 3 | Maximum anti-aliasing passes, and therefore the top of `aa_strength`. One pass removes 34% of a stair-step, three removes 64% while still keeping 70% of the edge. `pad_for` multiplies **both** AA terms by this and pins it rather than deriving it from the strength, for `_SAND_PASSES`' reason: `pad_for` runs at the un-supersampled scale and must not disagree with the renderer about the count. |
| `_AA_DIR_K` / `_AA_DIR_MIN` | 0.5 / 0.7 | Tangent-estimate blur for AA, as a fraction of its radius, and a floor. Smaller than `_SAND_DIR_K` against a smaller radius — this filter follows a contour at the pixel scale, and a wide estimate window cuts the corners off small features. The floor is what stops the tangent swinging on single-pixel noise. |
| `_SMOOTH_MAX` | 0.5 | Peak global-grain smoothing sigma as a fraction of the clump. Half a cell takes measured gridiness 1.74 → 0.27, so the lattice is gone rather than softened, while clump-scale structure survives. |
| `_SMOOTH_GAIN_K` | 5.62 | Restores the amplitude that smoothing blur costs, as `sqrt(1 + k(σ/cell)²)`. Analytic because a measured `std()` would be a statistic of the region and would seam exports. **Fit against the two-octave field it is used on** — calibrated on single-octave noise it comes out 7.7 and makes full Smoothness 10% louder than none. |
| `_GRAIN_ROT` | atan(2/(1+√5)) = 31.717° | Tilt of the Global Grain point lattice against the pixel grid. An irrational slope makes the two grids incommensurate at *every* cell size, which is what removed the pixel-grid resonance outright (std spread 3.4% across ten sizes, against a 35% amplitude hole at whole numbers). It replaced a domain warp and is cheaper than it — four multiplies instead of a `_value_noise` call — and it is the only thing that can de-align a lattice-addressed field at all: rotating *value* noise merely rotates its quilt. |
| `_GRAIN_SLOTS` / `_GRAIN_FILL` | 3 / 0.62 | Candidate points per cell, and the fraction of slots holding one — a count of Binomial(3, 0.62) per cell, mean 1.86. One point per cell is a stratified process whose density cannot vary, which is what read as an even mesh when zoomed out. Swept 2–5 slots at matched density: near-identical, so this is the cheapest count that still varies. 3 slots over a 3x3 search is 27 evaluations, what the old 5x5 single-slot search already cost. |
| `_GRAIN_RINGS` | 1 | Neighbour-cell rings the search checks each way (3x3). **Exact, not a heuristic**: an excluded point is strictly more than 1 cell away, no radius can exceed 1 cell, and the falloff is zero at the radius. Pinned against a 5x5 reference at 2.7e-07. Full-cell jitter is what the removed warp bought back. |
| `_GRAIN_SHARE` | 3 | Exponent weighting each candidate's brightness in the readout. A hard argmax leaves a cusp wherever two overlapping grains change places, and it could flip winners on a last-bit difference between tile layouts; a weighted mean has no winner to flip (tile independence 2.6e-04 → 1.2e-06) and needs no tie margin. Not a plain sum — the sum is normalised, and amplitude still comes from the single largest falloff, so a gap stays exactly a gap. |
| `_GRAIN_CLUSTER_CELLS` / `_OCTAVES` / `_ROUGHNESS` | 6 cells / 3 / 0.7 | Shape of the multi-octave field modulating each grain's brightness — the only thing in the construction that gives the layer structure above one clump, and therefore the answer to "repetitive when zooming out" (local-contrast spread 0.026 → 0.152). Three octaves because a single pitch gives every clump-of-clumps the same size, which is a different repeating pattern rather than none. Pitched in *cells*, so the mottling scales with the clump. **How deeply it modulates is no longer here** — it is `global_mottle`, since 2026-08-13. |
| `_GRAIN_CLUSTER_REF` | 0.6 | The depth `_GRAIN_STD_FIT` was fitted at, and therefore the depth `_grain_gain`'s mottling correction is anchored on — **not a default**, since `global_mottle` ships at 0. It is the number that reproduces the layer as it stood before that slider existed, verified at max abs diff 0.00e+00 against a pre-change render, which is the only reason it still needs a name. Originally chosen by sweeping 0.4/0.6/0.8 with the depth fixed: 0.4 invisible at a distance, 0.8 reading as patchiness. What that sweep missed is that 0.6 is already over the line on a *smooth* subject — 4.4% of the amplitude envelope surviving a blur to 33px, against ~2% for an even field. The right depth depends on the picture, so it cannot be one number. |
| `_GRAIN_CLUSTER_VAR` | 0.196 | `Var(2m − 1)` for the cluster field, which is what holds `global_mottle` to texture instead of loudness. **Derived then confirmed, not fitted**: `camp` has mean 1, so `E[camp²] = 1 + depth²·V` and the field's sigma is its square root. Uncorrected the field runs 6.0% louder from depth 0 to 1 — the size of effect that reads as "this made the grain stronger". Predicts the measured sweep over 0…1 to 0.30% at the extremes; the residual is grains reaching one pixel sharing correlated `camp` values, so the expectation is not quite exact. |
| `_GRAIN_TARGET_STD` / `_GRAIN_STD_FIT` | 0.29 / cubic in Min÷Max | Amplitude normaliser, so `global_intensity` means one thing at every size. The two old constructions disagreed by 43%. Closed form for invariant 1 — a measured `std()` normalises each tile differently — and it *can* be closed form because the point pattern in cell units is scale-free, so the std depends on the ratio alone (pitches 4, 8, 16 agree to 1.4%; the cubic fits to 0.12%). |
| `_JITTER_MAX` | 3.0 | Peak edge displacement in full-res px at `edge_jitter` 1. Was an inline 0.6, whose *typical* displacement was 0.227px — invisible. |
| `_STEP_LO` / `_STEP_HI` | 0.030 / 0.110 | Luma-step bounds separating a real transition from fine texture, for the edge-softening mask. Fine texture measures an order of magnitude under a hard border, which is the gap that lets softening take the snap off a border and leave fabric alone. |
| `_TEX_LO` / `_TEX_HI` | 0.002 / 0.015 | Local mean-abs-deviation bounds separating "smooth" from "textured" for the smooth-area guard. Skin and clear sky sit at or below `_TEX_LO`; fabric, foliage and hair sit above `_TEX_HI`. |
| `_LEAK_PHI` | 0.618… | Golden step placing leaks around the perimeter. A low-discrepancy sequence, not a stratification, so leak *k* lands in the same place whatever the count is — raising the count must add a leak, not reshuffle the ones already on the frame. |
| `_LEAK_CORNER_BIAS` | 0.10 | Pull toward the ends of a border, as `t − bias·sin(2πt)`. **Must stay under 1/2π = 0.159** or the map folds and every leak collapses into a corner; measured slope −0.51 at 0.24. |
| `_LEAK_WARP` / `_LEAK_REACH_SAFETY` | 0.15 / 1.25 | Domain-warp peak as a fraction of a leak's reach, and the divisor on the reach cap that pays for it. The warp can carry the falloff inward, so the centre-fog cap has to cover both or the frame's centre stops being exactly zero. |
| `_LEAK_GAIN` | 2.0 | Exposure per unit of leak before `leak_strength`. Sized so a default-strength leak's core just saturates — that is what makes the core white and leaves the colour in the falloff. |
| `_WARM_AXIS` / `_WARM_GAIN` | (0.603, −0.078, −1.0) / 0.14 | The direction Highlight Warmth and Shadow Warmth push along, and how far ±1 pushes. The axis is a warm shift **projected onto the luma-null plane** — subtract its own luma from every channel and the luma weights sum to exactly zero, so the shift is a pure change of colour and cannot fight Shoulder or Brightness for the same range. Normalised on its largest component, so the gain reads directly as "how far the worst-shifted channel moves": 0.14 is 36 8-bit levels of blue. **Nearly three times the pair it replaced**, whose peak was 0.055 reached only at pure white — an ordinary highlight moved under two levels, which is why both controls were reported as doing nothing. |
| `_WARM_HI_BAND` / `_WARM_LO_BAND` | (0.30, 0.85) / (0.15, 0.70) | Where each end's weighting reaches full strength. They **overlap through the mid-tones on purpose** — disjoint bands leave an untinted stripe across the middle of the range. Widened from (0.45, 1.0) and (0.0, 0.5), which only reached full weight at pure white and pure black, so most of a real photograph took a fraction of the setting. |
| `_DUST_ECCENT` / `_DUST_HARMONICS` / `_DUST_ROUGH_SPREAD` | 0.35 / (0.24, 0.17, 0.12) / (0.45, 1.5) | A speck's peak eccentricity, and the amplitudes of the 3rd/4th/5th angular harmonics denting its outline **at `dust_irregular` = 1** — they are scaled by that slider, which ships at 0, so what ships is exactly the ellipse. Exact circles are the clearest tell a texture was generated, which is why eccentricity is not on the slider; past ~0.4 the specks start reading as short scratches. Harmonics start at the 3rd because the 2nd *is* an elongation and would only fight the eccentricity draw. **The sum is the number that matters** — the radius is `1 + Σ aₖcos(kφ+pₖ)`, so a sum at or above 1 folds the outline through its own centre; 0.53 × the 1.5 top of the per-speck spread is 0.795, the worst case any setting reaches. Raised from a fixed 0.22 on 2026-08-08, when the harmonics became a control: the top of a slider has to look different from its middle. |
| `_DUST_SIZE_SPREAD` / `_HAIR_LEN_SPREAD` | (0.55, 1.55) / (0.65, 1.4) | Per-mark spread about Dust Size and Hair Length. Debris does not come in one size; a population all cut to the slider's exact value reads as stamped. The dust draw is squared, so small debris outnumbers large — a flat draw puts as many 1.55× specks on a frame as 0.55× ones, which reads as gravel. |
| `_DUST_EDGE_MIN` / `_DUST_EDGE_MAX` / `_DUST_EDGE_PX` | 0.10 / 0.85 / 0.5px | A speck's edge width as a fraction of its own radius at Dust Softness 0 and 1, and an absolute floor in working pixels. The floor is not decoration: a 1px speck's 10% edge is a hundredth of a pixel, i.e. a hard step whatever the analytic profile says. **`_DUST_EDGE_MAX` stopped being a ceiling on 2026-08-08** — Dust Softness runs to 5 and the mapping stays linear past 1, reaching 3.85 radii at the top. It used to clamp there, which is precisely why the slider was reported as too weak at its maximum. Past an edge of 1 the inner smoothstep bound goes negative and the speck loses its solid core, which is the diffuse smudge badly out-of-focus debris actually is. |
| `_MARK_MIN_PX` | 0.5 | Narrowest half-width the pixel grid can carry. Below it a mark is drawn *at* the floor and faded by what is missing, rather than drawn thinner — which is what area-averaging would have done anyway. **A filament needs twice this**, a full pixel: a disc always has a pixel centre within reach of its own soft edge, a line can thread between them for its whole length and render as dashes. Measured before the fix: one hair came out as a 394px filament plus a detached 1px speck. |
| `_DUST_SOFT_FADE` | 0.45 | How much of a mark's opacity softness takes away. Out-of-focus debris really is both softer and fainter — the same light over a wider footprint — and at 0 the softness slider reads as "the specks got bigger". **Applied over the first unit of softness only**, which matters now that the slider runs to 5: extended linearly it crosses zero at 2.2 and the specks would delete themselves halfway up the new travel. Past 1 the widening profile dims its own peak, so the geometry carries the fade from there. |
| `_HAIR_CURVE` / `_HAIR_WOBBLE` / `_HAIR_SLOPE` | 0.45 / (0.18, 0.07) / (0.55, 0.30) | A filament's quadratic sag and two sinusoidal wobbles, as fractions of its half-length, and **the ceiling on each wobble's steepest lateral slope**, which is the one that matters. The renderer measures distance to the curve as the vertical gap over `sqrt(1+slope²)`, which is the perpendicular distance only while the curve is locally straight — a wobble steep enough to double back scores points genuinely on the curve against the wrong part of it and the hair comes out in pieces (a fifth of them did). Capping the *slope* rather than the amplitude lets a slow wobble be wide and forces a fast one to be shallow, which is what a fibre does anyway. |
| `_HAIR_WIDTH` / `_HAIR_TAPER` / `_HAIR_TIP` | 1.6px / 0.55 / 0.15 | Filament width at full resolution, where the taper starts as a fraction of the half-length, and how thin the tip gets. The width is inherited from the level-set construction this replaced, where it had to be *solved* for (a level set is `2·eps·cell` wide, and the first attempt at 0.35px drew literally nothing); drawn directly, the width is simply the width. |
| `_R2_A1` / `_R2_A2` / `_MARK_JITTER` | 0.7549 / 0.5698 / 0.06 | Reciprocal powers of the plastic number — the 2-D low-discrepancy step dust and hair are placed on, and `_LEAK_PHI`'s trick in two dimensions — plus how far a mark jitters off its slot. Any prefix of R2 is well spread, which is what lets mark 6 be added without moving marks 1–5. The jitter is fixed in frame units rather than scaled to the count: at 400 specks the R2 spacing (0.05) is under it so placement goes locally random, and at a count of three the spacing dwarfs it and the sequence wins. Scaling it to the count would move every existing mark whenever the count changed. |
| `_BLOB_CELLS_SCRATCH` | 26.0 | How many countable marks one cell's worth of thresholded area breaks into, for scratches. Purely a calibration constant — the geometric argument predicts ~1.3 and measurement says otherwise — and good to about a factor of 1.5. **The dust and hair versions of this are gone**, because both are drawn from lists now and their counts are exact. A fitted constant standing in for a count is evidence the construction cannot express what the slider claims. |
| `_MID_GREY` | 0.46 | Pivot for the (deferred) contrast section. |
| `_BLUR_EXACT_MAX_SIGMA` / `_BLUR_DECIMATE_TO` | 32 / 16 (primitives.py) | Sigma above which `_blur` drops to a decimated grid, and the residual sigma it aims for there. **The threshold is the safety argument**: `k = floor(σ/16)` is below 2 for every σ under 32, so everything below falls through to the exact convolution and is bit-identical — pinned from both sides in `verify.py`. 16 rather than a smaller target because the error is set by how much of the gaussian the resample approximates away: targets 4 / 8 / 16 measure 5.3% / 1.5% / 0.7% of peak at σ 80, and 16 is still ~5000× faster there on the CPU. Only reachable when the caller passes an absolute `origin` — without one the pool grid would be tile-relative and the export would seam (measured 6.35e-03 before that was fixed). |
| `_WORKING_BYTES_PER_PX` | 640 (device.py) | Peak render memory per *working* pixel, what `tile_for` sizes a tile against. **Only holds because `release_cache` runs between tiles** — measured 2026-08-08, a 24MP `Stock` export peaked at 27.2GB driver-allocated against 9.9GB of live tensors (1075 B/px) because the caching allocator was hoarding ~17GB of freed blocks. With them returned it is 559–629 B/px, and 640 sits above that. Raised from 512, which had fallen *under* the real figure by 17% — the direction that runs a machine out of memory rather than merely slowing it. Not constant in frame size (563–630 at a 2400px proxy), so set it from the export, which is the case that runs out. |
| `_TILE_BUDGET_SHARE` / `_GRAIN_CACHE_SHARE` / `_CHECKPOINT_SHARE` | 0.7 / 0.15 / 0.15 (device.py) | How `_render_budget_bytes` is split between the renderer and the two caches. They genuinely compete — a cache holds device tensors the renderer then cannot have — and the split used to be implicit: `tile_for` took the whole budget while the texture cache took a flat 0.5GB on top, so neither was a real ceiling. **0.7 to the renderer is the number that must not drop**: at 0.6 a 2400px proxy tips over into two tiles and `SuperPortra` measured 1.75s → 2.72s on the GPU, 7.9s → 9.5s on the CPU. A cache that makes the uncached path slower is a bad trade however good its hit rate, so the caches were sized around the renderer's share rather than out of it. 0.15 each is what they need: five texture layers are 922MB at a 2400px proxy and 1.6GB at a 24MP export tile; a checkpoint is 184MB per boundary per tile. Generation-aware eviction keeps both live sets to two parameter states, so these caps bound the worst case rather than the usual one. |
| `_RELEASE_MIN_SHARE` | 0.5 (device.py) | How large one tile's working set must be, as a share of the tile budget, before `release_cache` is worth its synchronise between tiles. Ungated it fires on tiny tiles where there is no peak to bound and only costs the next tile a re-acquire — `verify.py` went 35.7s → 96.4s. At half the budget the suite is back to 36.1s while every tile large enough to matter still releases (a 24MP export tile is 100% of the budget; an 8GB machine's proxy tile is 171% of its own). |
| `MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES` | 30MB (imageio.py) | Input cap. JPEG/PNG only (`INPUT_FORMATS`, which also accepts `MPO` — a multi-frame JPEG; cameras emit it for burst and 3D and the file is still a .jpg). Not arbitrary — it is what keeps a full-resolution-per-keystroke preview affordable. |

`global_intensity` shares `_AMP_SCALE` with the main intensity slider but is
weighted by no mask, so the same number bites roughly 2.2× harder: measured on
flat 0.5 grey, 32 gives 7.7% luminance sigma where the masked slider gives 3.5%.
Response is exactly linear in intensity and in opacity, and the two multiply
(100 × 0.2 measures identically to 20 × 1.0). Do not "fix" the mismatch by
rescaling — the honest mapping is that this layer is unmasked; the help text
says so and points at 5–20 as the usable range.
